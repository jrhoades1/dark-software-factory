# Tone Matching Patterns

Examples of how to match communication tone from real interactions.

## Pattern: Warm-Brief (Jackie Rafter style)

**Their message:**
> We're looking forward to this too Jimmy.
> Erin will be sending over some stuff for you today.
> And best of luck with your job interview... Let me know how that goes!
> Jackie

**Match with:**
- Short sentences, warm but not effusive
- Personal touches (she remembered the interview — reciprocate with personal details)
- First-name sign-off only
- No formal signature block
- Exclamation marks are OK (she uses them)

**Good response:**
> Thanks Jackie! Really appreciate the encouragement. Interview went well — I'll fill you in when we meet.
> Excited to dig into the Journal.
> Jimmy

**Bad response:**
> Dear Jackie,
> Thank you for your kind words regarding my upcoming interview. I will certainly update you on the outcome.
> Best regards,
> Jimmy Rhoades

## Pattern: Professional-Practical (Erin Wilkins style)

**Their message:**
> Hi Jimmy,
> It was great chatting with you yesterday too. It looks like you have some amazing skills when it comes to using AI.
> I'm not sure if Jackie mentioned, but we get anyone working with us and accessing our materials to sign an NDA. I've attached one for you here.
> Thanks again,
> ERIN WILKINS
> Chief Operating Officer

**Match with:**
- Slightly more formal than Jackie but still friendly
- Full name greeting ("Hi Jimmy" not just jumping in)
- Process-oriented (she handles logistics — acknowledge that)
- Compliment noted but not over-reciprocated
- "Thanks again" closing style

**Good response:**
> Hi Erin,
> Thanks for sending this over — signing now. And thanks for the kind words about the AI work. Really enjoyed the conversation yesterday.
> Jimmy

## Pattern: Quick-Efficient (action items)

When someone sends materials or completes a task, respond fast and brief.

**Their message:**
> Perfect, thanks Jimmy. I've attached the Career Transformation Journal.

**Match with:**
> Got it, thank you! I'll dig in and have something to show you soon.

## Anti-Patterns

### Over-grateful
> Thank you SO much for taking the time to meet with me. I really, truly appreciate the opportunity and I'm incredibly grateful for your consideration.

**Why it's bad:** Puts you below them. You're collaborators, not supplicant and benefactor.

### Over-formal after casual
> Dear Ms. Rafter,
> Per our conversation of March 4th, 2026...

**Why it's bad:** They signed "Jackie." You write "Ms. Rafter." Instant distance.

### The essay
> I wanted to follow up on our conversation and share some additional thoughts I had about the project scope, timeline, and potential integration points...
> [400 more words]

**Why it's bad:** They'll skim it. Or not read it at all. Say it in 3 sentences.

### The non-follow-up
> Just wanted to touch base! Hope you're doing well.

**Why it's bad:** No content, no next step, no reason for them to respond.
